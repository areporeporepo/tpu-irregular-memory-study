◆Finding2026-08-16 11:06:47

A TensorCore row gather of 1M indices from a 4.2M-row table runs at 1.3 GB/s, which is 0.08% of the chip's 1,600 GB/s of HBM bandwidth.

This is the headroom the whole study is about. Gemini's read: HBM transaction granularity means a random small-row read pulls a wide line and discards most of it, predicting sorted or block-contiguous indices could recover roughly 10x. Untested.
✕Correction2026-08-16 11:06:47

The first fabric numbers were my own harness, not the fabric. A 0.55 ms floor appeared identically for two different collectives at four payload sizes, which is the signature of host dispatch.

Fixed by chaining 32 collectives inside one jit and dividing. Corrected all_to_all at 0.25 MiB: 0.027 ms of fabric under 0.53 ms of dispatch. The uncorrected figure was 20x too high.
◆Finding2026-08-16 11:06:47

Host dispatch costs 0.51 ms at 0.25 MiB rising to 1.23 ms at 16 MiB, up to 20x the cost of the collective itself.

Scale-independent and actionable: small collectives on TPU are orchestration-bound rather than network-bound. This is what an on-chip collectives engine like TPU 8i's CAE exists to remove.

Measured on a v6e-32 in us-east1-d, median of 20 samples, dispatch amortised.
✕Correction2026-08-16 11:06:47

My host-boundary explanation for the +77% is probably wrong. Gemini proposes bisection congestion, which fits better.

16 chips is likely a 4x4 torus and 32 a 4x8. The bisection cut stays 4 links wide while injected data doubles, so the same physical bottleneck carries twice the traffic. Test: run a 16-chip sub-communicator on the 32-chip slice at identical payload. If it stays fast, the effect is scaling and congestion rather than slice shape.
◆Finding2026-08-16 11:06:47

A collective costs 1.37x to 1.62x more while the MXU is busy: 1.62x at 8 chips, 1.61x at 16, 1.37x at 32.

This is the number that matters to anyone running thousands of chips, because step-time models are built from idle microbenchmarks. Tail was tight, p99/p50 = 1.00, on a single-tenant slice.
