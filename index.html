✕Correction2026-08-16 11:09:05

The +77% inflection I attributed to a host boundary was wrong, and the 256-chip run proves it. The same 16 chips cost 0.239 ms when carved from a 32-chip slice and 0.421 ms when carved from a 256-chip slice.

Same payload, same participant count, 1.76x apart. So the cost is set by the physical geometry the sub-communicator sits in, not by N. This is the sub-communicator control experiment Gemini proposed, performed accidentally by running the same sweep on two slice sizes. Gemini's bisection-congestion explanation survives; mine does not.
◆Finding2026-08-16 11:09:05

Carving a sub-communicator out of a slice is penalised, and using the whole slice is not. all_reduce at 0.25 MiB costs 0.208 ms on 128 of 256 chips but only 0.056 ms on all 256.

3.7x, in favour of the full slice. Likely XLA selecting a different collective algorithm when the participant set matches the native topology. Directly actionable for anyone partitioning a pod between jobs: a job whose chips do not form a natural sub-torus pays for it in every collective.
◆Finding2026-08-16 11:09:05

Small all_reduce degrades with participants while all_to_all does not. At 0.25 MiB, all_reduce goes 0.066, 0.113, 0.208 ms across 32, 64, 128 chips, while all_to_all goes 0.037, 0.040, 0.047.

Latency-bound reductions grow with chip count; bandwidth-bound exchanges stay flat. The two primitives have opposite scaling characters, which is a clean argument for why training and inference silicon diverged.

◆Finding2026-08-16 11:06:47

A TensorCore row gather of 1M indices from a 4.2M-row table runs at 1.3 GB/s, which is 0.08% of the chip's 1,600 GB/s of HBM bandwidth.

This is the headroom the whole study is about. Gemini's read: HBM transaction granularity means a random small-row read pulls a wide line and discards most of it, predicting sorted or block-contiguous indices could recover roughly 10x. Untested.

◆Finding2026-08-16 11:06:47

Host dispatch costs 0.51 ms at 0.25 MiB rising to 1.23 ms at 16 MiB, up to 20x the cost of the collective itself.

Scale-independent and actionable: small collectives on TPU are orchestration-bound rather than network-bound. This is what an on-chip collectives engine like TPU 8i's CAE exists to remove.

✕Correction2026-08-16 11:06:47

My host-boundary explanation for the +77% is probably wrong. Gemini proposes bisection congestion, which fits better.

16 chips is likely a 4x4 torus and 32 a 4x8. The bisection cut stays 4 links wide while injected data doubles, so the same physical bottleneck carries twice the traffic. Test: run a 16-chip sub-communicator on the 32-chip slice at identical payload. If it stays fast, the effect is scaling and congestion rather than slice shape.
